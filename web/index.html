What we learned

  • Normalization helps every method. Double Metaphone rises from F1 0.777 to 0.823 and trigram from 0.767 to 0.808; exact matching finds 33% of duplicates instead of 7%.
  • Phonetic codes are the most precise. Double Metaphone on normalized names is right 94% of the time it says "duplicate", but misses about a quarter of real duplicates.
  • Hard negatives are the real test. Different people with similar names fool every method: even the best Hybrid flags 39% of them, trigram 54%.
  • Random pairs are never confused. No method made a single false positive on them, so all the difficulty is in look-alike names.

Note: the Hybrid weights, and every method's threshold, were tuned on these same 15,586 pairs, so the scores are optimistic. A held-out set would give a fairer estimate.

Speedup at 100k records

Double Metaphone gains less because the GIN index only narrows the table to trigram candidates: every candidate must still be split into words and given a phonetic code, and that per-row work, which the index cannot skip, is most of its query time.
